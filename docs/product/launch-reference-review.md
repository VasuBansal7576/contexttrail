Reviewed 4 October 2026. This records external feedback and a proposed direction for the next ContextTrail film. It is not a release acceptance record or a new video export.

## What was inspected

I inspected its opening, middle and ending with the visible player, sought through the timeline at roughly three-second intervals, and checked continuous playback. The existing seven-scene storyboard repeats the same entry/reveal/hold instructions for each scene. It also moves between a news number, unresolved clip research, saving and watching. The next film should carry one question through to its evidence and a saved result. Each transition should follow a change in the investigation.

Use the authored ContextTrail casebook throughout: the actual chapter layouts, Instrument Serif, Geist, paper, brick, blue, source papers and engraved hand. The HTML remains the frontend authority. The reference film informs storytelling and motion; it does not replace that visual direction.

## Motion and interaction decisions

- Move the viewer's attention from the question to the discovered source, then to the retained passage. Each camera move or reveal must explain that relationship.
- Keep quotations, dates and assessment text still while they are read. Adjust hold lengths using the rendered film at its intended viewing size.
- Use brief motion between decisions and longer pauses on evidence. Avoid an identical animation recipe in every chapter.
- Make the engraved hand point at the exact source, quotation or next action under discussion. Verify its target in every final frame size.
- In the companion, let the viewer pause on a source, read its retained text and deliberately open the original. An attractive transition must not remove inspection time.
- Choose the final invitation around an action the viewer can perform immediately. Show the actual question entry rather than a decorative closing screen.
